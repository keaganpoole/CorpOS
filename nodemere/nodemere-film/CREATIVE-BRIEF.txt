# NODEMERE

# Cinematic Product Introduction

## Complete Creative Direction & Production Brief for Codex + Remotion

You are acting as the **creative director, senior motion designer, cinematographer, and lead Remotion developer** for a premium cinematic product introduction video for Nodemere.

This is not a traditional advertisement, tutorial, screen recording, or explainer video.

This is a sophisticated, visually striking introduction to Nodemere and its features, drawing inspiration from Apple's highest-quality product films, especially its kinetic typography, meticulous microanimations, beautifully choreographed interface presentations, cinematic camera movements, and precision in visual storytelling.

The finished video must feel intentionally directed, exceptionally polished, and visually memorable.

**Your objective is not simply to animate Nodemere's interface. Your objective is to present Nodemere as though a world-class motion design studio were revealing an extraordinary new product.**

You have access to the Nodemere codebase. Study it extensively before building anything.

The instructions below constitute the complete creative vision for the project.

---

# 1. THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

## Direct the product. Do not redesign the product.

Think of Nodemere as an exceptionally beautiful, highly detailed luxury sports car that has already been meticulously designed and perfected.

Your job is to create an extraordinary cinematic presentation of that car.

You can change the camera angle, create dramatic close-ups, make the car float in an environment, present it from different perspectives, isolate details, move around it, and choreograph its appearance.

**You cannot redesign the car itself.**

This same principle applies to Nodemere.

Nodemere already has sophisticated components, typography, colors, layouts, hover states, motion, microinteractions, animations, transitions, and other visual details.

Many of these took substantial effort to develop and perfect.

They must be preserved.

### You are allowed to:

- Move, rotate, tilt, scale, isolate, and position existing UI components within cinematic compositions.
- Use three-dimensional perspective to make elements appear to float independently in space.
- Create sophisticated camera movements around existing elements.
- Magnify interfaces dramatically to emphasize important details.
- Animate the appearance and disappearance of entire elements.
- Reveal sections through cascading, staggered, masked, or spatial transitions when appropriate.
- Temporarily adapt the outer dimensions of a component for video presentation when necessary.
- Create new hero typography, icon animations, backgrounds, transitions, and visualizations specifically for the video.
- Simulate product interactions using appropriate demonstration data.
- Arrange components on a cinematic stage without reproducing all the surrounding dashboard chrome.
- Use matching cuts and carefully designed transitions to connect different product features.

### You must not:

- Redesign existing UI components.
- Change existing fonts, colors, borders, radii, shadows, gradients, internal spacing, or general layout.
- Replace sophisticated existing animations with approximations.
- Invent new versions of existing modals, panels, menus, or interactions.
- Recreate complex visual components from memory when the real implementation exists.
- Alter the underlying appearance of receptionist portraits or videos.
- Introduce generic styling that makes Nodemere appear different from the real product.
- Simplify complex interfaces merely because reproducing them accurately is inconvenient.

**The outside can be cinematic. The inside must remain Nodemere.**

If the original component has a particular flicker, ripple, cascade, gradient sweep, or transition, find and reuse the original implementation.

Do not decide how you would animate it if that animation already exists.

Study how Nodemere actually animates it.

This rule is especially important for the Scenarios Builder, People CRM, Nest, Audition portraits, receptionist collection, calendar, and Drop-ins Builder.

# 2. CREATIVE VISION

## The desired feeling

The video should be:

- Bold and dramatic.
- Sophisticated and contemporary.
- Extremely visually polished.
- Cinematic and dimensional.
- Beautifully restrained where restraint makes sense.
- Energetic and attention-grabbing where impact is appropriate.
- Deliberate and highly creative in its animation.
- Consistent with Nodemere's visual identity.
- Clearly understandable, even without narration.

The goal is to produce something that feels considerably more refined than a typical SaaS launch video.

We want the precision of Apple's product cinematography, translated into a software-first experience.

### The Apple inspiration

The reference is not Apple's hardware.

Do not introduce mock iPhones, fabricated devices, or phone frames simply because Apple uses them.

The inspiration is Apple's **cinematic design language**:

- Beautifully controlled 3D perspective.
- Elegant product isolation.
- Dramatic scale transitions.
- Close-ups of tiny details.
- Sophisticated transformations between compositions.
- Large, exquisitely animated typography.
- Detailed, purpose-built icon animations.
- Smooth, physical-feeling camera movement.
- Excellent timing and easing.
- Strategic visual contrast.
- Thoughtful negative space.
- Precise timing between quiet and impactful moments.

The attached Apple reference recordings and charging-icon screenshot, if available to you, are inspiration for movement quality and craftsmanship, not instructions to recreate the content.

The charging icon is particularly important as an example of restrained sophistication. Its internal fill animation makes the icon itself interesting without surrounding it with excessive effects.

## Avoid generic AI-generated motion design

We absolutely do not want the visual clichés commonly produced by AI coding agents.

Avoid:

- Excessive glow.
- Random particle explosions.
- Unnecessary orbiting shapes.
- Constantly pulsing UI.
- Generic bounce-in animations.
- Cheap neon effects.
- Overdone glassmorphism.
- Unmotivated spinning elements.
- Excessive floating decorations.
- Repetitive scale-and-fade transitions.
- Motion simply for the sake of motion.
- Tacky or outdated visual effects.

This does **not** mean everything should be subtle.

We want ambitious animation that can command attention.

But sophistication should come from composition, internal detail, choreography, timing, and precision, not from piling on decorative effects.

**Be visually ambitious, not visually noisy.**

# 3. NODEMERE'S VISUAL IDENTITY

Nodemere is fundamentally a dark-mode product.

Its primary design language consists of:

1. Near-black or black backgrounds.
2. Soft white or light-gray typography.
3. A restrained pink-to-purple brand gradient.

These are descriptive references, not literal color values.

## Find the real colors

The Nodemere brand gradients and colors are defined in `index.css`.

Inspect the existing project and identify the actual brand variables and styles.

Use the authentic values.

Do not invent replacement colors.

### Video backgrounds

Use predominantly black or nearly black backgrounds.

The video should resemble a sophisticated dark cinematic stage.

Some subtle gradients or lighting variations are acceptable when they enhance the composition, but do not overpower the product.

Do not alternate to white backgrounds without a compelling creative reason.

### Hero typography

Large, bold, clean, highly legible.

The majority of the text should be white or the project's existing off-white typography color.

The Nodemere pink-purple gradient should be used sparingly, for specific emphasized elements.

### Accent color philosophy

One excellent opportunity for brand color is the animated icon incorporated into hero statements.

The icon can feature the pink-purple gradient while the surrounding typography remains largely white.

This creates a memorable visual identity without excessive color.

Existing UI elements must retain their original colors, even if they differ from this simplified hero-statement palette.

# 4. HERO STATEMENTS

Throughout the video, features should be introduced using large, isolated typographic moments.

We will refer to these as **Hero Statements**.

A Hero Statement is a cinematic title composition, often appearing against a dark background, that briefly tells viewers what they are about to see.

For most feature sections, the Hero Statement should appear **before** the feature demonstration.

We specifically chose this order because it gives the audience context before presenting a sophisticated interface.

This prevents viewers from having to guess what they are looking at.

However, the opening cold open is a deliberate exception.

## Hero statement design

Imagine:

A black background.

Beautifully arranged, enormous white typography.

One intricately animated icon integrated directly into the statement.

Perhaps a single word receives a subtle emphasis.

No clutter.

No unnecessary decoration.

The composition should be instantly readable on desktop, tablet, and mobile.

### Important: the icons are miniature works of motion design

These icons are not basic SVGs that simply fade or bounce into view.

Each icon should receive its own carefully choreographed reveal sequence.

The icon's animation may contain several coordinated stages.

For example, an icon might:

1. Reveal its silhouette through an internal mask.
2. Animate its internal geometry.
3. Fill with a controlled brand gradient.
4. Complete a subtle mechanical or physical transformation.
5. Settle into its finished resting position.

These stages should feel like one cohesive movement, not several unrelated tricks.

Each icon should have a distinct animation tailored to its meaning.

Examples:

**Intercom / Phone:** A telephone symbol could assemble its internal contours, undergo a beautifully timed localized vibration, and settle.

**Languages:** A language-related icon could transition between carefully crafted character forms or speech glyphs through internal shape transformations.

**Payments:** A payment symbol could reveal its shape and smoothly transition into an approved or completed state.

**Security:** A lock or cryptographic structure could assemble through intricately synchronized internal components, ending in a strong, perfectly aligned final form.

The icon animation should be predominantly **self-contained**.

Its sophistication should happen inside its shape and immediate boundaries.

Do not surround it with excessive particle fields, rings, glowing debris, or unrelated effects.

A small amount of light or glow is acceptable if visually justified.

### Animation behavior

Each hero icon should have one carefully developed reveal.

Afterward, it should settle into a calm resting state.

Do not leave every icon continuously animating.

### Text wording

Favor short, direct, meaningful product statements.

The viewer should understand what the feature does.

Avoid vague, abstract copy that sounds sophisticated but communicates nothing.

For example:

Prefer **"Create your own receptionist"** over **"One of a kind."**

Use the approved wording specified later in this document wherever available.

For unspecified statements, propose concise options and use provisional text until approved.

# 5. CINEMATOGRAPHY

This is one of the defining elements of the entire video.

Nodemere contains many dense interfaces.

The Nest is very small.

CRMs have numerous columns.

The calendar contains relatively small appointment records.

Scenario-building interfaces contain substantial detail.

If these are displayed as ordinary screenshots, they will lose much of their appeal.

**Cinematic magnification is essential.**

## Extreme close-ups

Use dramatic push-ins and controlled zooms that bring individual components, labels, buttons, records, or animations into sharp focus.

For example:

We begin with a large floating CRM table.

The camera tilts slightly.

Then it rapidly pushes toward a particular column.

The column becomes enormous in the frame.

The camera slowly tracks across its illuminated Zone.

A user interaction occurs.

The camera glides with the interaction.

Then we pull back, shift perspective, and dive toward another part of the CRM.

These movements should resemble physical cinematography.

Not ordinary browser zooming.

## Perspective

Use sophisticated 3D transformations when they add value.

Examples:

- Slightly angled interface panels.
- Gentle rotational movement.
- Perspective changes during transitions.
- Screens floating against the dark stage.
- Moving between foreground and background elements.
- Camera paths that move across or toward features.

Avoid excessive perspective distortion that makes UI unreadable.

## Every feature needs its own choreography

Do not give every feature exactly the same sequence of:

Fade in → zoom in → tilt → zoom out → fade out.

The general cinematic language should be consistent, but each scene must have its own personality.

Some shots should move quickly.

Some should glide slowly.

Some should remain nearly motionless while an existing Nodemere interaction performs.

Some should pull dramatically backward.

Some should reveal components through coordinated transitions.

The camera should respond to the feature.

**Use motion to direct attention.**

# 6. TECHNICAL PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS

## Production technology

Use Remotion with React and TypeScript, integrated with the existing Nodemere project as appropriate.

First inspect the repository, installed packages, available Remotion-related skills or plugins, existing components, styling, assets, and application architecture.

Use appropriate libraries where they genuinely improve the result.

Prefer a well-organized Remotion project with reusable primitives for:

- Camera animation.
- Perspective transformations.
- Scene transitions.
- Hero typography.
- Icon animation.
- Cinematic staging.
- UI presentation.
- Deterministic demonstration data.
- Timeline management.

Use frame-based deterministic timing.

All animations must be reproducible when rendering arbitrary frames.

Avoid relying on unpredictable real-time state, unmanaged timers, or live service calls during the final render.

## Output

Primary master:

**3840 × 2160, 16:9, 60 fps.**

Produce a high-quality 4K master suitable for YouTube and website embedding.

If rendering resources make full 4K development inefficient, use lower-resolution previews during iteration and render the approved sequence at final resolution.

Maintain sharp text, smooth motion, clean gradients, and excellent temporal consistency.

## No audio

**This production is completely silent.**

Do not add:

- Music.
- Narration.
- Sound effects.
- UI clicks.
- Telephone sounds.
- Generated voices.
- Audio snippets.

Audio will be handled separately afterward.

This includes the Intercom, Audition, language demonstrations, and Handoff sequence.

Visual waveform animations and simulated transcripts are still allowed and encouraged.

Do not rely on sound to make a scene understandable.

## Absolutely no pixelation or blur from enlargement

This requirement is non-negotiable.

The video will contain dramatic close-ups.

Do not simply take low-resolution screenshots and magnify them.

Use the actual UI rendering wherever practical.

Render DOM, text, SVGs, and vector-based elements at sufficient source resolution so that they remain sharp at the largest intended on-screen scale.

Where a component must be enlarged, consider rendering it in a sufficiently large isolated composition rather than magnifying a small raster capture.

Account for camera scaling, perspective, and the component's effective pixel density.

For high-resolution assets, use original source files at adequate resolution.

For receptionist videos or raster images, inspect their actual resolution before using extreme close-ups.

Do not assume that 4K output automatically makes low-resolution source footage sharp.

If an asset cannot withstand the intended magnification, obtain a better source, reduce the magnification, or adjust the framing without modifying the component's design.

Do not apply aggressive sharpening as a substitute for real detail.

At close-up scale, inspect for:

- Pixelation.
- Blurry text.
- Raster artifacts.
- Aliasing.
- Distorted borders.
- Soft icons.
- Unreadable labels.
- Damaged image quality.

## Preserve Nodemere's real UI

Whenever possible, import and reuse the actual React components and existing styles.

Do not build visual imitations of components that already exist.

If components depend on authentication, Supabase, ElevenLabs, telephony events, or application state, build isolated demonstration adapters that provide deterministic data while preserving the existing visual implementation.

You may need to adapt the environment in which a component renders, but do not compromise its design.

Keep production application behavior separate from video-only demonstration behavior.

Do not mutate real customer data, trigger actual phone calls, send actual payments, or contact live customers while generating the video.

Use fictional demonstration records.

Never display sensitive production data.

## Quality control

Use Remotion previews, rendered frame captures, and browser-based inspection to evaluate the result.

Inspect the scene at multiple points rather than assuming the code produces a good animation.

Pay attention to:

- Timing.
- Easing.
- Camera composition.
- Visual hierarchy.
- Text readability.
- Sharpness.
- Transition continuity.
- Perspective distortion.
- Existing component fidelity.

The video should look excellent in motion, not merely in isolated screenshots.

# 8. SCENE ONE: THE NEST COLD OPEN

## Importance

The beginning of the video is exceptionally important.

The first five seconds must receive disproportionate creative attention.

A weak opening will undermine the entire production.

We do not necessarily want to begin with something loud or overwhelming.

The desired progression is:

**Subtle introduction → intriguing human moment → extraordinary collection reveal.**

## Opening composition

Begin against Nodemere's dark cinematic environment.

Introduce the actual Nest component.

The Nest is normally a small, narrow, Dynamic Island-like element positioned in Nodemere's top toolbar.

Because of its compact size, it must be presented substantially larger in the video.

Do not show the full dashboard at a distance and expect the Nest to be visible.

Instead, isolate and magnify the Nest using cinematic staging and camera movement.

The actual Nest component must retain its internal design, styling, and animation.

A video-specific outer container adaptation is acceptable where needed.

For example, the compact Nest may smoothly expand downward to show its larger conversation state.

## Simulated Intercom call

Use an actual receptionist from the existing Nodemere receptionist catalog.

Simulate the Intercom selecting that receptionist and initiating a call.

The visual sequence should include:

- The receptionist's real avatar or portrait.
- The original Intercom call-state visuals.
- The existing call animation.
- The receptionist banner.
- The live waveform.
- The live transcript.

The call is simulated for the video.

Do not actually place a telephone call or require live ElevenLabs execution.

## Conversation

Use a very short, naturally conversational exchange.

The receptionist's punchline should be something similar to:

**"Oh, not much. Just chillin' at the front desk."**

The line should feel casual, slightly unexpected, and human.

Use an appropriate preceding caller prompt if needed to make the reply logical, but keep the interaction brief.

Because this video is silent, the live transcript must communicate the exchange visually.

The exact transcription wording can be refined while constructing the scene.

## The big transition

This is the first major cinematic moment in the production.

When the receptionist's response appears, bring the camera dramatically closer.

The text containing **"chillin' at the front desk"** should become a focal point.

Push in extremely close until the conversation occupies much of the frame.

Then use that movement to launch into an extraordinary transition into the Receptionist Collection.

The transition should feel powerful, beautifully timed, and spatial.

Do not use an ordinary fade or cheap digital explosion.

The idea is that the camera propels us out of the intimate Intercom conversation and into the much larger world of Nodemere's receptionists.

The collection reveal should be the true opening spectacle.

Spend significant effort perfecting the choreography between these two moments.

# 9. SCENE TWO: RECEPTIONIST COLLECTION

## The concept

Nodemere contains a Receptionist Collection.

This is a navigable, brick-like canvas of receptionist thumbnails.

Users can pan around the canvas, explore receptionists, and select individual receptionists to view more information.

This scene should make the collection feel enormous, beautiful, and full of personality.

## Cinematic progression

Start relatively close to a small cluster of receptionist thumbnails.

The perspective should make these portraits feel impressive and substantial.

Then rapidly pull the camera backward.

As the camera retreats, reveal an increasingly large collection of receptionists.

Use a snappy, well-eased camera movement.

The feeling should be a sudden revelation of scale.

The viewer should think:

*There are so many different receptionists to choose from.*

The collection is the primary spectacle.

## Hero statement

A large title should land at approximately the moment the full collection reveal settles.

We previously decided that synchronized timing between the pullback and title would be more effective than introducing all the text too early.

The final collection headline can be refined, but it should directly communicate choosing or discovering receptionists.

Avoid vague statements.

## Navigating the collection

After the initial reveal, show some cinematic movement across the collection.

For example:

- A controlled lateral glide.
- A subtle perspective shift.
- A camera push toward an interesting receptionist.
- A change in depth or framing.

Do not simply display the collection as one static screenshot.

## Selecting a receptionist

Focus on one real receptionist card.

Simulate selecting that receptionist.

The actual Nodemere receptionist modal should open.

This modal contains the receptionist video, appearance, personality information, and related details.

**Preserve its existing typography, design, colors, spacing, and composition.**

You may animate the modal's overall entrance or create a video-specific cascading reveal if doing so does not alter the underlying design.

Allow viewers to appreciate the receptionist's appearance and details.

Use existing video assets when available.

# 10. SCENE THREE: AUDITION

## Narrative transition

Audition should be integrated naturally with the Receptionist Collection.

The collection demonstrates choosing an existing receptionist.

Audition demonstrates creating one yourself.

After showcasing the collection, transition to another hero statement.

Suggested text:

**"Or create your own."**

Alternatively, use the more explicit:

**"Create your own receptionist."**

Clarity takes priority.

## Audition overview

Audition is Nodemere's custom receptionist creation studio.

It is a multi-step flow in which the user chooses characteristics such as:

- Name.
- Gender.
- Available appearance or background options.
- Personality.
- Tone.
- Prompt customization.
- Voice.
- Receptionist image.

The studio eventually generates three voice options through ElevenLabs, allows users to select a voice, and creates the receptionist.

The completed receptionist appears in the Receptionist Collection.

## Important visual element: dynamic portraits

Audition features large, detailed portraits that change according to user selections.

This is among its most important visual elements.

Study the exact image implementation.

The portraits must remain:

- In their original positions.
- At their intended relative dimensions.
- Consistently aligned.
- Visually faithful to the source assets.
- Smoothly transitioned using the original implementation.

The current portrait system was deliberately designed so that changing selections does not cause the portrait to jump or reposition.

Preserve that behavior.

**Do not recreate the faces, reinterpret them, or invent new face-blending transitions.**

Use the existing assets and source behavior.

## Camera treatment

Audition is more like a beautifully designed form or studio.

Do not force the same extreme zoom treatment used in the CRM.

Instead, let the interface's own visual storytelling carry the sequence.

Move between steps with elegant continuity.

Use restrained perspective and transitions where appropriate.

Spend extra attention on the portrait changes.

They should feel like an impressive transformation taking place within an anchored composition.

## Voice selection

When the three generated voice options appear, show their existing play controls.

Simulate selecting and previewing multiple voices.

Their existing animated waveforms should play.

There is no actual sound.

The viewer should understand the interaction through the interface's visual response.

Avoid reinventing the waveform.

## Final creation moment

Show the image-selection stage.

Then demonstrate finalizing the receptionist.

The newly created receptionist should transition into the existing Receptionist Collection.

This creates a satisfying narrative loop.

The viewer has moved from browsing receptionists to creating one of their own.

# 13. SCENE SIX: THE CONVERSATION NETWORK

## This is an original motion-graphics sequence

Unlike the CRM or Scenarios Builder, this sequence does not need to recreate an existing product screen.

It is a custom visualization of Nodemere's AI receptionist capabilities.

It should therefore be one of the more creatively ambitious parts of the video.

## Central concept

Imagine a beautiful, living network of conversations.

At its center is an actual Nodemere receptionist.

Her video or portrait is contained within a circular mask.

She is the central point of the network.

From this receptionist, elegantly designed branches extend outward.

At the ends of these branches are small circular customer avatars.

Each customer represents an individual conversation.

The overall structure is inspired by a tree, where the receptionist acts as the trunk or base and the customers act as leaves.

However, this should not look like a literal cartoon tree.

It should resemble a meticulously designed, abstract, intelligent conversation network.

## Opening movement

Start very close to the receptionist's face.

The camera pulls back.

A branch appears, connecting the receptionist to a customer.

Another branch appears.

Then another.

As the number of conversations increases, the camera continues retreating to accommodate the growing structure.

The movement should feel spatially intentional.

Additional branches should emerge from the central structure, not unrealistically grow from customer avatars.

The network becomes increasingly expansive.

The result should be visually impressive, highly organized, and easy to understand.

## Feature one: multiple conversations

Approved hero text:

**"One Receptionist. Multiple Conversations in Parallel."**

This can be divided across lines for visual composition.

The network should establish the receptionist simultaneously serving multiple customers.

Do not claim unlimited conversations.

There are actual system limits, and we do not want inaccurate messaging.

## Inbound and outbound calls

Some customer connections represent inbound calls.

Others represent outbound calls.

Use small, elegant directional indicators.

For example:

An arrow pointing toward the receptionist represents inbound.

An arrow pointing away represents outbound.

Make this visual distinction easy to understand.

At an appropriate point, reveal:

**"Inbound & Outbound Calls."**

The indicators should integrate naturally into the network design.

Avoid clutter.

## Feature two: payments

Approved hero text:

**"Take Payments Right on the Phone."**

Keep the conversation network visible.

Introduce small, tasteful payment indicators next to selected customer avatars.

These indicators should be beautifully designed and consistent with the rest of the sequence.

Some can transition into a successful payment state.

Avoid generic bouncing money symbols or cartoon coin animations.

The effect should visually communicate that the receptionist can assist with payments during calls.

Do not imply payment capabilities beyond those supported by Nodemere's actual implementation.

## Feature three: languages

Approved hero text:

**"Auto-Detect and Speak 75+ Languages."**

This is intended to represent the receptionist's ability to recognize language changes and respond in another supported language during a conversation.

Verify the current supported language count and technical behavior against the actual Nodemere/ElevenLabs configuration before finalizing this claim.

Transition the customer indicators into language-related visuals.

Possible visual methods:

- Small language indicators near different customers.
- Country flags where appropriate.
- Carefully chosen language glyphs.
- A subtle localized transformation of text.

Be mindful that languages do not always correspond directly to countries.

The feature should feel multilingual and adaptive.

No audio.

## Feature four: appointment booking

Suggested text:

**"Book Appointments Automatically."**

Alternatively:
